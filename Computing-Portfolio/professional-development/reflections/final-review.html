Project Overview

This portfolio was produced as part of the BSc (Hons) Computing Level 5 programme. Its purpose was to document four weeks of independent study across web technologies, data structures and algorithms, networking and cyber security, and artificial intelligence with professional development.

The project combined academic research, technical explanation, comparative analysis, reflective writing and website development. The final product therefore provides evidence of subject knowledge while also demonstrating my ability to organise and present a substantial body of work through a structured digital portfolio.

Achievement of the Project Aim

The principal aim was to develop an accessible portfolio that demonstrated independent learning across several areas of computing. I consider this aim to have been substantially achieved because each weekly section contains focused notes, supporting terminology, comparisons, reflective discussion and academic references.

The website format provided a suitable method of presenting the work because it allowed the content to be divided into manageable pages while retaining clear navigation between related topics. It also enabled me to apply practical HTML and CSS skills rather than submitting the research only as a conventional written report.

However, completion of the content alone does not automatically demonstrate that every aspect of the project was equally successful. The portfolio is strongest as an organised record of research and reflection. Some topics could be strengthened further through additional practical implementations, test evidence and technical artefacts.

Knowledge and Understanding Developed

The project broadened my understanding of how different areas of computing connect. Week 1 demonstrated that web development depends on more than page design because DNS, HTTP, HTTPS, servers, databases and browser behaviour all contribute to the operation of a web application.

Week 2 developed my understanding of efficiency and data organisation. Studying algorithms, complexity and databases showed that technical decisions must be based on the requirements of a problem rather than on a preference for a particular method.

Week 3 established stronger connections between networking, cloud computing and cyber security. It demonstrated that the availability and convenience of connected services must be balanced against risks relating to confidentiality, integrity, reliability and dependence on external providers.

Week 4 extended the study into artificial intelligence, ethics and professional development. This highlighted that computing decisions can have social and organisational consequences. Technical ability must therefore be supported by ethical judgement, awareness of bias and a commitment to continued learning.

Strengths of the Portfolio

One of the principal strengths is the breadth of the subject coverage. The portfolio considers software, infrastructure, data, security and emerging technology rather than treating computing as a single narrow discipline. This provides evidence of a developing understanding of the relationships between different technical areas.

A further strength is the organisation of the material. Weekly landing pages, consistent navigation and separate content pages make the portfolio easier to use than one continuous document. The shared styling also creates a more coherent visual identity across the project.

The portfolio demonstrates improvement through revision. Earlier pages were reviewed for academic tone, structure, referencing and clarity. This iterative process reflects a more realistic development approach than treating the initial version as complete.

Areas for Improvement

The main limitation is that some subjects are represented primarily through written explanation rather than through practical implementation. For example, the portfolio discusses algorithms, databases, cyber security and artificial intelligence, but these areas could be supported by additional code examples, test results or small technical projects.

The referencing also required repeated checking. Although academic sources were used throughout the project, consistency across in-text citations and reference lists needs careful management. A formal referencing audit before submission would reduce the risk of missing details or inconsistent formatting.

Project planning could also have been more systematic from the beginning. Some time was spent restructuring folders, navigation and page layouts after content development had already started. A clearer initial specification, project plan and file structure would have reduced unnecessary revision.

Testing was completed throughout development, but the evidence was not always recorded formally. A structured checklist covering links, responsive behaviour, accessibility, browser compatibility and content accuracy would make the quality-assurance process more transparent.

Skills Developed

The portfolio strengthened my ability to research unfamiliar technical topics independently. I had to identify relevant sources, compare explanations and decide how much detail was appropriate for a Level 5 audience.

My technical writing also improved. The work required me to explain concepts clearly without oversimplifying them. This was particularly important when discussing topics such as algorithmic complexity, TLS, cloud service models and machine learning.

Developing the website improved my HTML and CSS skills, especially in relation to reusable page structures, relative file paths, navigation and responsive layout. It also demonstrated the importance of accessibility features such as semantic headings, alternative text and clear link labels.

The project also developed transferable skills including time management, self-evaluation, problem-solving and attention to detail. These skills are relevant to both academic study and professional computing practice.

Future Development

The next stage of development should focus on converting more of the researched knowledge into practical evidence. A small web application using JavaScript, input validation and documented testing would extend the work completed in Week 1.

Further study in databases, cyber security and cloud computing would also be useful. Practical laboratories and small projects could help connect theoretical understanding with implementation and troubleshooting.

I also intend to improve project-management practice by using milestones, risk assessments and test plans from the beginning of future projects. This should reduce unnecessary restructuring and provide clearer evidence of progress.

The SWOT analysis and SMART targets developed during Week 4 provide a basis for reviewing this progress. These targets should be monitored and updated when sufficient evidence has been produced or when professional priorities change.

Final Evaluation

Overall, the portfolio has met its central purpose by demonstrating independent study, technical understanding and reflective development across four computing themes. The website is not only a record of completed academic work, but also evidence of my ability to organise, revise and present information in a structured digital format.

The strongest outcomes are the breadth of research, the connections made between technical subjects and the improvement in the quality of the portfolio through revision. The principal areas for further development are practical implementation, formal testing and more efficient project planning.

Completing the portfolio has increased my confidence in independent learning and has clarified the skills that I need to develop further. It therefore provides a useful foundation for future academic work and continued professional development within computing.
