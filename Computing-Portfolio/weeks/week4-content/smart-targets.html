Purpose of the Targets

SMART targets are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. The following targets respond directly to the development needs identified in my SWOT analysis.

Together, they prioritise completion of the current portfolio while also supporting longer-term development in practical web programming, academic practice, cyber security, project management and career planning.

SMART Framework

S Specific Defines exactly what will be completed.
M Measurable Identifies evidence that will demonstrate progress.
A Achievable Remains realistic within available time and ability.
R Relevant Supports academic or professional development.
T Time-Bound Includes a clear completion point.
Portfolio Completion Web Development Referencing Cyber Security Planning and Testing Career Direction
1

Complete and Quality-Assure the Portfolio

Immediate priority Portfolio submission September deadline

S Specific

Complete all outstanding portfolio pages and carry out a final review of content, navigation, accessibility, spelling and referencing.

M Measurable

Every required page will contain completed content, all internal links will work, images will have suitable alternative text and the site will contain no known missing files.

A Achievable

The main weekly content is already developed, so the remaining work can be completed using a structured quality-assurance checklist.

R Relevant

This directly supports the quality, accessibility and reliability of the final Level 5 portfolio submission.

T Time-Bound

Complete the final quality-assurance review before the planned September submission date.

Evidence of Completion

Completed pages, validated links, accessibility checks, image-alt-text review and a final portfolio checklist.

Deadline

Before the September portfolio submission.

2

Improve Practical Web Development Skills

Practical project HTML, CSS and JavaScript Eight weeks

S Specific

Build one small interactive web application using semantic HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

M Measurable

The application will include at least one form, input validation, dynamic page behaviour and a documented testing table.

A Achievable

The scope will be limited to a manageable application such as a task tracker, quiz or study planner.

R Relevant

This will strengthen the practical evidence supporting my existing front-end development knowledge.

T Time-Bound

Complete and document the application within eight weeks of submitting the portfolio.

Evidence of Completion

Working application, source code, screenshots, validation evidence and a completed testing table.

Deadline

Within eight weeks of portfolio submission.

3

Strengthen Academic Referencing

Academic practice Harvard Cite Them Right Pre-submission audit

S Specific

Review all in-text citations and reference-list entries against the Harvard Cite Them Right format.

M Measurable

Every source cited in the text will appear in the reference list, and every listed source will be cited in relevant portfolio content.

A Achievable

The review can be completed one weekly section at a time using a consistent referencing checklist.

R Relevant

Accurate referencing improves academic integrity, credibility and the overall standard of assessed work.

T Time-Bound

Complete the referencing audit at least two weeks before submission.

Evidence of Completion

Completed reference checklist, matched citations and references, corrected formatting and verified access dates.

Deadline

At least two weeks before submission.

4

Develop Cyber Security Knowledge

Technical development Security pathway Three months

S Specific

Complete an introductory cyber security learning pathway covering common threats, access control, network security and incident response.

M Measurable

Complete every required learning unit and produce a one-page reflective summary of the knowledge gained.

A Achievable

The pathway will be selected at an introductory or intermediate level appropriate to my current experience.

R Relevant

Cyber security applies across computing roles and extends several topics already studied in this portfolio.

T Time-Bound

Complete the pathway within three months of portfolio submission.

Evidence of Completion

Completion record, course notes and a one-page reflective summary explaining the knowledge developed.

Deadline

Within three months of portfolio submission.

5

Improve Project Planning and Testing

Project management Risk and testing Next project

S Specific

Use a project plan, risk register and formal testing checklist for my next substantial computing project.

M Measurable

Documentation will include milestones, deadlines, identified risks, mitigation actions and recorded test outcomes.

A Achievable

Templates can be prepared in advance and updated throughout the project rather than created retrospectively.

R Relevant

This addresses weaknesses in time management, scope control and systematic quality assurance.

T Time-Bound

Apply the documentation from the first week of the next assessed project.

Evidence of Completion

Project schedule, risk register, test plan, recorded outcomes and evidence of milestone reviews.

Deadline

From the first week of the next assessed project.

6

Clarify a Future Career Direction

Career planning Three pathways Six weeks

S Specific

Compare three potential computing career pathways and identify the technical skills, qualifications and experience expected for each.

M Measurable

Produce a comparison table and select one pathway for more focused development.

A Achievable

The comparison can be based on current job descriptions, professional-body guidance and course learning.

R Relevant

A clearer career direction will help prioritise future projects, training and professional-development activities.

T Time-Bound

Complete the comparison within six weeks of portfolio submission.

Evidence of Completion

Career-comparison table, skills-gap analysis and a written explanation of the selected direction.

Deadline

Within six weeks of portfolio submission.

Monthly Review Process

Progress against these targets should be reviewed monthly. Each review should record whether the target is on schedule, what evidence has been produced and whether any adjustment is necessary.

Targets should only be changed where circumstances or learning priorities have genuinely changed, rather than because progress has become difficult.

Check Progress

Compare completed work with the target deadline.

Record Evidence

Identify documents, projects or results produced.

Identify Barriers

Record problems affecting completion or quality.

Update Actions

Adjust the plan where a justified change is needed.

Conclusion

These SMART targets convert the broad findings of the SWOT analysis into practical and measurable actions. They prioritise completion of the current portfolio while also supporting longer-term development in web programming, academic practice, cyber security, project management and career planning.

Connection to the SWOT Analysis

These targets were developed from the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats identified in the personal SWOT analysis.
